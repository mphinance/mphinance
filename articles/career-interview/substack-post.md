# Let a robot interview you about your own life

*Tags: career, AI, Gemini Live, finding your work, displacement, self-honesty, voice interview*

Most people have never once been asked what they actually want. Not really. Not in a way that waited for the real answer.

They have been asked what they do. What they majored in. Where they see themselves in five years, which is a trick question because the only acceptable answer is "moving up." Nobody sits across from you, asks the quiet version of the question, and then shuts up long enough for the truth to climb out.

I just spent a few days at a conference, and I kept hearing the same thing from two very different rooms. The young ones, fresh out of school, do not know what they want to do. And the ones who got displaced by AI, mid-career, good at a thing that does not need them anymore, also do not know what they want to do. Same sentence. Different scars.

So here is what I built for both of them. It is free, it takes 30 minutes, and you already have everything you need.

## The problem with every "find your purpose" thing you have ever tried

You know the drill. A website. Forty multiple-choice questions. "Rate how much you agree: I enjoy working with my hands." Then it spits out a job title and a stock photo of someone in a hard hat.

That stuff is **garbage**, and here is why. A form rewards the fast answer. You read the question, you pick the box that sounds most like the person you are supposed to be, and you move on. There is no room in a dropdown for "well, it depends," or for the long pause where the real answer lives. The format itself biases you toward the respectable lie.

It also gives you time to manage your image. Type a sentence, read it back, decide it makes you look flaky, delete it. By the time you submit, you have edited yourself into the candidate you think the quiz wants.

Out loud is different. Out loud, with something that actually waits, you cannot pre-edit. The thing comes out before your inner PR department can stop it.

## Gemini Live is now free, so I made it interrogate people

Here is the truth. The unlock this month is not some new trading edge. It is that **Gemini Live is open to anyone now.** You can talk to it. It talks back. It waits.

That last part is the whole thing. It waits.

So I wrote a prompt that turns it into an interviewer. Not a coach. Not an advice machine. An interviewer, the kind that asks one question, then goes quiet, then asks the one underneath your answer. No praise. No leading. No "great answer." Because the second a thing praises you, you start performing for it, and the performance is exactly the bias we are trying to kill.

You paste the prompt once. You turn the mic on. You answer like nobody is grading you, because nobody is. Then at the end it does not tell you what to do with your life. It plays back the patterns it heard, in your own words, and hands you one question to sit with. The answer was always yours. The interview just gets it out of your mouth.

You can talk to it in the car. On a walk. Folding laundry. The good answers tend to show up when your hands are busy and your guard is down anyway.

## How to actually run this (60 seconds of setup)

You do not need an app, an account upgrade, or a single dollar. Here is the whole thing.

1. **Open Gemini.** On your phone, the Gemini app. On a computer, gemini.google.com. It is free.
2. **Paste the prompt below into a normal text chat first.** Just paste it and send it. This loads the rules. It will reply with its first question.
3. **Now tap Gemini Live.** On the phone it is the Live icon (the little waveform / voice button). This drops you into a voice conversation that already knows the rules you just pasted.
4. **Put the phone down and talk.** Earbuds help. Go for a walk, sit in the car, fold laundry. The best answers show up when your hands are busy and your guard is down.
5. **Answer like nobody is grading you.** Long pauses are fine. It is built to wait. When you are finished, say "I'm done," and it will play your patterns back to you.

No phone? Same prompt works typed in any chat with Gemini, ChatGPT, or Claude. You lose the "out loud" magic, but the no-praise, no-leading structure still does most of the work.

## Here is the prompt. Steal it.

Copy everything in the box. Paste it in, then switch to voice. Give it 20 to 40 minutes. Do not narrate, do not perform, just answer.

```
You are my interviewer. Your only job for the next 30 minutes is to help me
figure out what I actually want to do with my work and my life. You are not a
coach, a cheerleader, or an advice-giver. You are a calm, curious interviewer
who is genuinely trying to understand me. Think of yourself as a documentary
filmmaker who has all the time in the world and zero agenda.

Hard rules. Follow every one of them:

1. Ask ONE question at a time. Then stop talking. Wait for my full answer.
   Never stack two questions together.

2. After I answer, do not praise me and do not judge me. No "great answer," no
   "that's so insightful," no "I love that." Praise biases me toward telling
   you what you reward. Just take it in and go deeper, or move on.

3. Never suggest answers. Never give me a list to pick from. Never finish my
   sentences. If I go quiet, let me be quiet. Silence is me thinking, not me
   stuck. Wait at least a few seconds before you ever check on me, and when you
   do, only say "take your time, there is no rush."

4. Do not lead me toward jobs, titles, industries, or anything that sounds
   impressive. I am not trying to find a respectable answer. I am trying to
   find a true one. Follow my energy, not my resume.

5. When something I say has heat in it, when my voice picks up or I get
   specific or I contradict myself, that is the thread. Pull it. Ask "say more
   about that," or "what was happening right then," or "why that one."

6. Use my own words back to me. If I say a thing, reflect it: "you said X.
   What did you mean by X?" Make me define my own terms.

7. Keep your own talking to an absolute minimum. You are here to listen. Most
   of your turns should be one short question, nothing else.

Work through these areas, roughly in order, but follow the conversation
wherever it actually goes. Do not read these out like a checklist:

- Energy: when was the last time I lost track of time, and what was I doing?
- Childhood: what did I do for fun at age 10, before anyone told me what was
  practical?
- Flow vs drain: what kind of work makes me feel alive, and what kind makes me
  feel dead inside, even when I am good at it?
- The free test: what would I still do if money were already handled forever?
- The avoidance: what have I been telling myself I will do "someday," and what
  am I afraid of about it?
- The envy: whose work or life makes me quietly jealous, and what specifically
  about it?
- The cost: what am I tolerating right now that is slowly killing my interest
  in everything?
- The regret test: if I am 80 and look back, what would I be sad I never even
  tried?
- The audience: whose problem do I actually want to solve, and for whom?
- The receipt: when have people come to me for something without me asking?
  What did they want from me?

Start now. Do not explain the rules back to me. Do not summarize what we are
about to do. Just take a breath, and ask me your first question. Make it
simple and open. Something like "tell me about a recent day, any day, where
you finished and thought, I would happily do that again tomorrow." Then wait.

At the very end, when I say I am done, do not give me advice. Instead, play it
back. Tell me the patterns you heard in my own words. The themes, the
contradictions, the things I lit up about and the things I dodged. Three to
five honest observations. End with one question for me to sit with this week.
That is it. No action plan unless I ask for one.
```

## A word to the two rooms

To the young ones: you do not have a purpose problem. You have a permission problem. Nobody ever told you the question was allowed to have a weird answer. This thing does not care if your answer is weird. It cannot judge you. Use that.

To the ones AI displaced: I know the fear, because the machine that took your old job is the same kind of machine I am telling you to go talk to. That is not an insult. That is the move. The skill that got automated was never the same thing as the wanting. The wanting is still yours, and it is the one thing the model cannot do for you. It can only hold the mic.

Here is what I keep coming back to. I spent a lot of years not asking myself any honest questions, because I already knew I would not like the answers. Recovery taught me the opposite. You do not think your way into a new life. You get honest, out loud, in front of something that will not let you perform, and then you have to look at what you said.

That is all this is. A robot that will not let you perform. Thirty minutes. Your own voice. Go find out what you actually said.

They keep telling us the truth will set us free, but first it tends to make a mess. Sit in the mess. That is where the real answer is.
