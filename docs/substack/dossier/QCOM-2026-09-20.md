## The story

**What happened Friday:** not a headline. Qualcomm said its socket share in upcoming
iPhones lands materially below the prior ~20% assumption, and guided Apple product
revenue down **~50% sequentially** into the December quarter as Apple's in-house C2
modem ramps faster than Qualcomm modeled. Skyworks and Qorvo fell with it — it traded as
a sector move, not a single-stock event.

**The Amazon deal (announced 9/8/2026):**
- Up to **$60B** of Qualcomm AI data-center silicon, systems and services, 2026–2036
- Custom inference accelerators (AI200 ships 2026, AI250 in 2027) plus optical
  connectivity to **1.6 Tb/s+**, built on the Alphawave SerDes/DSP IP
- Warrants: **25M shares at $161.26** (~$4B), vesting in tranches tied to commercial
  milestones and the purchase volume
- Revenue starts Q1 FY2027 (Dec 2026). Targets: **~$5B FY27, ~$15B FY29**
- It **supplements** Trainium and Inferentia. It does not replace them

**The part to hammer:** $60B is a *ceiling on qualifying payments that trigger warrant
vesting*, not an order and not a backlog. And 25M shares on 1.068B outstanding is
**2.34% dilution** — Qualcomm is paying a customer in equity to buy its chips.

**Do the margin math on the page.** FY25 was $44.3B at 54.2% gross margin. Data center
runs low-to-mid 30s / low 40s. Blend $15B of 35%-margin revenue onto the base and the
consolidated gross margin goes **54.2% → ~49.3%**. Revenue +34%, gross profit +22%,
five points of margin gone. That's the LongYield point and it's the honest one.

**Other live threads:**
- **Snapdragon price hike:** double-digit percentage increase on anything shipped after
  **Sept 1, 2026**. A margin lever nobody is talking about — and a reason customers
  accelerate their own silicon
- **Arm:** Qualcomm won outright (Delaware, verdict Dec 2024, final judgment Oct 2025).
  Arm is appealing. Qualcomm's own countersuit tries **March 2026**. The Oryon cores are
  the data-center engine, so this is not a side issue
- **Auto:** design-win pipeline **$45B**, ~40 automakers, Ride Flex in mass production on
  eight programs
- **Humain** (Saudi PIF): **200MW** of AI200 racks starting this year

## Position sizing — the Tharp teaching section

This is the part that makes the article work for someone who doesn't care about Qualcomm.
Use a **$100,000 account** and tell readers to divide if theirs is smaller.

**Step 1 — R is what you lose if you're wrong.** Entry $176.36, stop $167.39, so
**R = $8.97 per share**. On this setup R is exactly one ATR, and that's not luck — the
stop was placed one average day's range under the entry band.

**Step 2 — R picks the share count.** Shares = (account × risk %) ÷ R.

| Risk | $ at risk | Shares | Position | % of account |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5% | $500 | 55 | $9,700 | 9.7% |
| **1.0%** | **$1,000** | **111** | **$19,576** | **19.6%** |
| 2.0% | $2,000 | 222 | $39,152 | 39.2% |

**The counterintuitive bit, and the reason to write this section:** risking 1% of a $100k
account means buying nearly **20% of the account in stock**. Most people hear "1% risk"
and size ten times too small, or they buy a round 100 shares and have no idea what they
just risked. The stop sets the size. Nothing else does.

**Step 3 — score the targets in R, then ask what win rate each needs.** This is where
Tharp collapses into the $190 argument and the whole piece clicks:

| Target | R-multiple | Break-even win rate |
|---|---|---|
| $180 — TD Pro T1 | 0.41R | **71.1%** |
| $185 | 0.96R | 50.9% |
| **$190** | **1.52R** | **39.7%** |
| $193.47 — EM high | 1.91R | 34.4% |
| $200 — TD Pro T2 | 2.64R | 27.5% |

Taking profit at $180 means being right **seven times out of ten, forever**, just to break
even. At $190 you can be wrong six out of ten and still make money. That is the same
conclusion the R:R table reached, arrived at from the opposite direction.

**Step 4 — the two numbers you know before clicking.** At 1% / 111 shares: **+$1,514** if
$190 hits, **−$996** if stopped. One trade cannot hurt you. That's the entire point.
