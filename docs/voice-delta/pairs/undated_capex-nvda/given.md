*Trading 50% | Macro 30% | AI 20%*

I'm not here to tell you to panic. Neither is he, and the reason is about 10% of everything he invests.

## What happened

Alphabet had its worst day since it went public in 2004, down about 13%. Amazon fell, then fell harder after the bell. Both said the same thing: we're spending even more on AI next year. Google is spending $195 to $205 billion this year. Amazon is spending about $220 billion and says next year will be "meaningfully more." Both are now burning cash faster than they make it.

The market heard "more spending" and sold the spenders.

!> Nobody on TV said this part. <span style="color: gold">**Capex is somebody else's revenue.**</span> Every dollar Amazon and Google spend lands in another company's sales line.

## Follow the money

So I followed it. Take every $100 billion that Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Meta spend on AI, and here's roughly where it lands. The dollars are also the percents.

![](03-follow-100b.gif)

- **$40B** goes to buildings, power and cooling.
- **$27B** goes to <span style="color: green">**Nvidia**</span>.
- **$22B** goes to the rest of the servers: CPUs, storage, assembly.
- **$6B** goes to custom chips, like Google's TPUs.
- **$5B** goes to networking and optics.

Broadcom gets paid twice, once for Google's TPUs and again for the switches that wire every data center together.

> The math on Nvidia: it booked $48.7 billion from the big cloud companies in its last quarter. The big four are spending roughly $175 billion a quarter. That's about 25 to 30 cents of every dollar. The $6B and $5B slices are rougher estimates; the Nvidia number comes straight from its filing.

## Why he's not panicking

About 10% of what Michael invests goes into Nvidia. So when he saw the spenders getting spanked, the question wasn't "how bad is this." It was "where's that money going." And the answer was sitting right there.

The number that actually lets him sleep: Only about 55% of Nvidia's data center revenue comes from the big cloud companies. The other $40.3 billion a quarter comes from AI clouds, governments and companies buying their own, and that part grew 138% in a year.

So I ran the stress test. Say Wall Street bullies Big Tech into cutting AI spending in half tomorrow. Nvidia keeps <span style="color: green">**73%**</span> of its data center revenue. The chart at the top of this post is that math.

|> The market punished the spenders. Nvidia's other customers didn't get the memo.

## The honest part

This isn't a "nothing can go wrong" post. If the spending really does get cut, Nvidia's revenue drops too, just less than the companies that live on one or two cloud customers. <span style="color: red">**Owning the supplier isn't a hedge against the spending. It's a bet that it keeps happening.**</span> And the custom chips, Google's TPUs and Amazon's Trainium, exist specifically to buy less from Nvidia over time.

Still. When the biggest companies on earth say "we're going to spend more," and you own the cash register they're spending it at, today's red candle reads different.
