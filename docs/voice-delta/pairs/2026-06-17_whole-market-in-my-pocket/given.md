# The Whole Market, In My Pocket

*I built a button that does my homework. Then I built a calculator that tells you when you can fire your boss.*

For years my morning was twelve browser tabs and a quiet panic. Screeners in one, options flow in another, some 13F PDF I'd pretend to read with my coffee. By the time I had an opinion, the move had left without me.

Not anymore. Now my phone buzzes once and the whole market is already chewed up and laid out in front of me. Two things this week. Let's get er done.

## The market just shows up on my phone now

I press one button, from anywhere, and a couple minutes later the entire market lands in my pocket as one tiny daily review. Poof.

It reads the screeners, the unusual options flow, what the big funds bought last quarter, the charts coiling up tight toward a breakout, and whether the market's nerves are calm or fried. Then it bolts on my own brokerage account, so the review actually knows what I'm holding.

![The morning review, real names and all](review-card.png)

It doesn't dump a hundred tickers on me. It hands me the short list where a bunch of signals that don't even talk to each other all happen to point the same way. I don't want a trade every screener agrees with. I want a trade none of them can break.

Now the confession. This isn't a Bloomberg terminal humming in a closet. It's [TraderDaddy Pro](https://www.traderdaddy.pro/?ref=8DUEMWAJ)'s brain, which my dossier borrowed one morning and quietly never gave back. The skill itself doesn't know a thing. TD Pro does. The skill is just the part that learned to text me. Bloomberg charges 24 grand a year for the version that doesn't.

And it's not really a big red button either. It runs itself every morning and I just poke it when I get impatient. Phone buzzes, intel appears, and I look like I did three hours of homework I very much skipped.

A screener does not pick trades. A pile of screeners that all agree does.

## What it actually flagged this morning

I'm not going to hype a machine and then show you nothing. This morning's tape: a regional bank convention.

USB, Truist, M&T, and Citi all popped the same setup on the same day, a momentum trend plus a chart that coiled up tight and finally broke out. Four boring banks, same move, same morning. Either something's coming, or all four read the same newsletter. I don't think it's the newsletter.

That's the part I care about. Two signals that don't talk to each other, landing on four names at once. One of those is noise. Four is a question worth asking. Regionals don't move as a pack unless something macro is under them: rates, the yield curve, money rotating back into what everyone left for dead. I don't know which yet. It's a flag on the calendar, not a position.

UNH had the cleanest chart of the bunch. META showed up on a different signal entirely, 13F data, which just means big funds owned it as of last quarter. That's lagged by definition, a "who else is in the room" note, not a "buy it now."

None of that is me yelling BUY. The machine points, I nod, and today it pointed at the banks.
