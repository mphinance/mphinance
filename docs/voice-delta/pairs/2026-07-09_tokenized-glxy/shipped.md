## The question nobody's asking
 launched tokenized stocks in 120-plus countries. Kraken has xStocks. Galaxy tokenized its own SEC-registered shares. Securitize rang the NYSE opening bell and tokenized itself the same day. This is not coming. It's here, and your feed is about to be full of it.

Then I read the fine print on one of them and my stomach dropped. Robinhood's "stock tokens" are not stock. They're debt securities issued through a shell company in Jersey. You are not an owner. You are lending money to a shell that promises to pay you whatever Apple does.

That is not a share. That is a bet on a share, wearing a share's clothes. 

## Three tiers, and only one is real

- 
- Everything getting sold as "tokenized stock" falls into one of three buckets.

Tier 1, the real thing. The token is the SEC-registered share. A registered transfer agent records your wallet as the shareholder of record. You get the votes, the dividends, the CUSIP, all of it. This is what Galaxy did. When the token moves, the legal owner-of-record moves with it. That is not a wrapper. That is the share.

Tier 2, backed but hollow. A custodian buys the real stock, locks it in a vault, and an offshore shell issues you a token against it. It's collateralized one-to-one, which sounds safe. But you own a claim against the shell, not the share. No vote. No rights. No legal claim on the company if it all goes sideways. This is Kraken's xStocks. Their own disclosure says it plainly: holding it "is not the same as buying a share."

Tier 3, pure synthetic. A debt note or a derivative that just tracks the price. No share exists in your name anywhere. This is Robinhood. This is Dinari's dShares. You own a promise, and the promise is only as good as the company making it.

"Backed by" is not "owned by." "Exposure to" is not "rights in."

## The one question that tells you which one you're holding
Ask this and nothing else. Does a registered transfer agent record my wallet as the shareholder of record?

If yes, you own the company. If the answer is anything with the words "custody," "backed one-to-one," or "special purpose vehicle" in it, you own an IOU from a stranger. Full stop.

## This already blew up. In May.

- 
- If you think I'm being dramatic, meet PreStocks.

This past May, a tokenized-stock outfit collapsed when the transfer of its underlying shares got ruled invalid. When they counted what was actually there, they found 23 million dollars in real shares sitting under tokens that traded like they were worth 1.3 trillion.

Twenty-three million holding up one-point-three trillion!

The token said you owned something. You owned air. Nobody set out to run a scam necessarily. The structure just let the paper multiply faster than the shares underneath it, and no one was checking in real time.

If you were around for crypto in 2014, you already know this movie. Mt. Gox. Everyone thought they owned Bitcoin. They owned an IOU from an exchange that turned out not to have the coins, and when it folded, 850,000 of them were just gone. PreStocks is Mt. Gox with a stock ticker. “Not your keys, not your coins” becomes “not your transfer agent, not your shares.” I’ve had hardware wallets for 10 years now.

## The problem nobody has solved yet
Now think bigger than one blown-up startup.

The entire passive-index machine, the trillions in money that just tracks the S&P and the Russell, runs on one assumption. One share, one place it trades, one official list of who owns it.

Tokenization quietly breaks all three. When a custodian locks a million real shares in a vault and a million tokens go trade on a blockchain, you now have two markets for the same share, on different clocks, with nobody reconciling them in real time.

So I went and read what the index providers said about it. MSCI, S&P, Russell, Nasdaq all rewrote their float rules this year. Every one of them was fixing exactly the number that tokenization corrupts. And not one of them said a single word about tokenized shares.

That silence is not comforting. That is a hole in the floor nobody's put a cone in front of yet. And I don’t tend to watch where I’m walking all the time.

## The pitch is not ownership. It's a stock that never sleeps.
Every real, Tier 1 name that tokenized did it for the same reasons, and none of them said "so people can own us." They said 24/7 trading, use-it-as-collateral, instant settlement.

That last part matters more than it sounds. I got curious about the "always open" claim because I use the overnight venues. Blue Ocean, the one most people mean when they say the market trades at night, runs 8pm to 4am Eastern, Sunday through Thursday. It's closed on weekends. Even the fancy new 24X exchange is 24/5. The plumbing that clears these trades only gets extended to 24/5 later this year.

A token on a blockchain is the only thing that genuinely trades 24/7/365. That's the real feature. It also cuts the other way, and nobody puts that on the brochure. No circuit breakers. Thin liquidity at 3am on a Sunday. A token can drift a mile from the real price when the actual market is closed and there's nobody awake to arbitrage it back.

Always working is also always exposed. It’s why I fell in love with crypto, but it’s a double-edged sword.

## While we're here: prediction markets are the same disease
Watch where the whole culture is drifting. Polymarket and Kalshi turned into a five-billion-dollar business, and people are calling it the future of information.

A prediction market share is the purest version of the thing I'm warning you about. You don't own a company. You don't even own exposure to a price. You own a bet on an outcome, priced between a penny and ninety-nine cents.

Tokenized Tier 2 and Tier 3 stocks, prediction markets, the whole wave. It's all the same move. Turn the actual thing into a position on the actual thing, and sell you the position. It trades great. It's liquid. It's fun. Just know that when the music stops, you're holding a ticket, not a chair.
