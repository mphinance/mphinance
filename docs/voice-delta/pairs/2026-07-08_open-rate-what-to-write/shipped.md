SUBTITLE: Business 50% | AI 30% | Mindset 20%
AUDIENCE: everyone

I updated a free tool that reads your Substack export and tells you what to write next.

Then I pointed it at my own numbers, and it told me three things I did not want to hear. All of them useful. None of them a guess.

## What the tool is
Substack hands you an export nobody actually reads. A zip with a pretty growth chart on top and, buried three folders down, 163 little files logging every open, every click, every subject line you ever sent. 44,393 rows of it.

So I wrote something that reads the whole thing. It runs entirely in your browser. Nothing uploads, nothing gets stored, you drop in your zip and it hands you a playbook. 

## Result one: your title is doing the opposite of what you think
Everybody tells you to put a number in your headline. "7 ways to." "3 mistakes." The listicle gospel.

My own data says that costs me points. Here is what actually moves the needle across my whole archive.

- 
- A $TICKER in the title is worth +7 points. A question mark, +4.6. And a number in the title? It costs you a couple points, the opposite of what every headline guide swears.

Look at the title of this post again. Ticker pun, question mark, no digits. I did not guess. I read the file.

## Result two: I am writing the wrong thing
I clustered every post I have ever written by topic and joined it to open rate.

My recovery and psychology writing, the getting-sober-mapped-onto-not-blowing-up-an-account stuff, opens best of anything I do. My AI and screener content, the tools I am proudest of building, opens worst.

- 
- Sit with that. I spend my energy on the machines. My readers show up for the confession. That is either a redirect or a fight worth having in public, and I honestly do not know which yet. But I would never have seen it without opening the ugly file.

## Result three: they decide in a day
Of the paying subscribers I could trace, the median gap between their last open and their first payment is 0.7 days.

They do not marinate. They read something that lands and they convert while it is still warm. So the ask belongs inside the post that is working, not in a drip sequence three days later when the feeling is gone.

That one changed how I think about the paywall more than any growth-hack thread ever did.

## The honest asterisk the tool made me swallow
One thing it flags that most creators never see: the majority of my "opens" are not people. They are Apple Mail and Gmail pre-fetching the tracking pixel the second the email lands, before a human touches anything.

So I read my open rate as a relative signal, post versus post, never as an absolute truth. The number is not fake. It is just measuring a machine's reflex and handing it to me like it measured your attention. The tool keeps me honest about that, which is the whole point of building your own instead of trusting the dashboard someone sold you.
