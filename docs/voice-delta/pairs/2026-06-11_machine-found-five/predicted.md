## First, respect the tape

Small caps are firm, not euphoric. The Russell 2000 is up about 4% since May 20. The moves you are about to see were not just a rising tide; these names actually outran their own index, which is the only reason they are worth a look.

Sectors are doing the talking. Semis are the leader of the whole year, up over 50%. Defense was dead all spring and just woke up in the last three weeks. Software is the laggard, actually red since May 20.

One more thing, and it matters today. The VIX sat asleep in the teens during this entire run, then popped from 16 to 22 today. Volatility just got out of bed. The market spent six weeks as a sleepy Sunday matinee. Today somebody yelled fire in the theater. Nobody is hurt yet, but everybody suddenly knows exactly where the exits are. Small speculative names are always first to bleed when that happens. Whatever you do here, size it small. This is a sleeve, not a savings account.

## The one I would run from: KULR

![](KULR_card.png)

My screener loved this thing. Tiny, ignored, "+98% revenue growth." It ranked it a rocket.

The screener is wrong, because it cannot read a 10-Q. I can.

KULR runs a *negative* gross margin. It loses money on every single unit before a dime of overhead. "Revenue growth" on top of that is not growth, it is faster bleeding. It is burning around 44 million a year against a cash pile measured in months. The share count has tripled. And somewhere along the way it quietly turned into a Bitcoin treasury stock, converting most of its cash to BTC and borrowing against the coins.

Every magician needs misdirection, and KULR found a gorgeous one. Watch the shiny Bitcoin in the right hand. Try very hard not to notice the left hand feeding the cash into a shredder.

So you are buying a cash incinerator wrapped around a levered Bitcoin bet. Twenty percent of the float is short, the most hated name on this list.

Sam ran the odds in her best C-3PO: the possibility of KULR navigating its cash runway without diluting you into oblivion is approximately 3,720 to 1. Never tell me the odds, you say. She just did. Run.

DNA score: 2.7 out of 10.

## The coin flip: GRRR

![](GRRR_card.png)

I have a soft spot here. I have made money on Gorilla. That is exactly why I am the wrong person to trust on it, and so are you if you are holding it.

Real revenue, 111 million and growing. Cash rich. A 2 billion dollar Supermicro deal that is genuinely exciting. And, on June 3rd and 5th, a fresh shelf registration and 107 million in convertible notes that knocked the stock down double digits. Real business, real dilution hanging over it.

It is also fighting its own sector. Software is the only group on this board that is red since May 20. Only 14% of the shares are held by institutions. The rest is you and me and the crowd, which is why it trades like a rollercoaster.

Sam's odds: 1 to 1. Even money it is a 2 billion dollar winner, even money the converts grind you to powder. A coin flip wearing a tuxedo.

DNA score: 5.8 out of 10.

## The pass: PDYN

![](PDYN_card.png)

Palladyne is a promise. Seven million in revenue, an operating margin of negative 337%, and the choppiest chart of the five. The story is great: defense autonomy, a missile propulsion contract, an Air Force space-swarm award. Insiders own a quarter of it, which I like.

But the chart is a tangle of EMAs with no clean trend, and you are paying a rich price for a backlog, not earnings. Well funded, real story, no setup. I am sitting on my hands.

Sam's odds: 5 to 1, and most of that is the story holding together.

DNA score: 5.8 out of 10.

## A genuinely good one, on the house: ASYS

![](ASYS_card.png)

Here is the rare thing in micro-cap land: a company that actually makes money. Amtech makes semiconductor equipment, runs a 48% gross margin, just printed a real profit, and grew revenue 31% with an AI advanced-packaging tailwind. The share count is basically flat. No dilution games.

And the smart money already knows. Sixty-five percent of the shares are held by institutions, including Izzy Englander's Millennium, sitting in a 390 million dollar company with a 10-million-share float. They cornered it. Almost nobody is short. When a quant billionaire quietly parks money in a company you have never heard of, that is the best chef in town eating at a diner with no sign out front.

My only knock is the chart. It is pinned to the top of its range, stuffed right against a ceiling it has rejected three separate times at 24 to 24-and-a-half. Great company, late entry. You would be chasing resistance here, so wait for the dip to the 18-and-change shelf or for a clean break above 24.50.

Sam's odds: 3 to 1. The only ones on this list she would actually take.

DNA score: 7.6 out of 10.
