# Getting Paid to Wait

*Options 50% | Screening 30% | Trading 20%*

The market is in a risk-off mood and most of my watchlist is too extended to chase. So I am not chasing. I am selling puts and getting paid to sit on my hands.

A cash-secured put is the most boring trade I know, which is exactly why it fits a week like this.

## How the wheel actually works

You pick a stock you would be happy to own. You pick a price below where it trades now. You promise to buy 100 shares at that price, and someone pays you cash today for the promise.

If the stock never falls to your price, you keep the cash and do it again. If it does fall there, you own a name you wanted, cheaper than it was Friday, and you start selling calls against it. That loop is the wheel.

In a flat or jumpy tape, you get paid while everyone else white-knuckles their entries.

## What the screener is doing

Sam runs my cash-secured put screener every morning. It hunts for names paying at least 1% a week on the cash you tie up, with the strike sitting around 10% below the current price, then it grades each one on whether the premium is actually worth the volatility you are taking on.

That grade matters more than the yield. A fat premium on a stock that is quietly falling apart is not income, it is a trap with a bow on it.

Worth noting: the screener tags the tape as risk-off this morning too. Same read as the rest of my work this week. When the model and the market agree on the weather, I dress for it.

## The catch nobody prints next to the yield

A 9% week is not free money. It is rent you collect for promising to buy a stock that can move 10% before lunch.

The premium is fat because the risk is real. Every name near the top of this list carries triple-digit implied volatility, which is the market telling you out loud that it expects big moves. You are being paid well because you are taking the other side of that fear.

So the only rule that matters is the one the screener prints at the bottom of its own page. Only sell puts on names you would actually want to own. Size it so an assignment does not wreck you. Start with the wider cushions. Roll down or out if the stock falls through your strike, instead of panicking.
