# Three Questions Before You Buy Any Pullback

*Three stocks. Same chart, basically. One is a buy. Two are traps. The difference is a filter you can run in ninety seconds.*

I almost bought all three of these today. Two of them would have been mistakes, and they would have looked smart right up until they weren't.

That is the whole problem with a pullback. A green chart near the highs is not a buy signal. It is a question. Most people answer it by squinting at the candles instead of asking the three things that actually matter.

I have been trading for a living about a year and a half now. Long enough to be not half bad at it, short enough that I still remember what every one of these mistakes costs. These three questions are most of what I learned in between.

![The filter: two of these names fail, one clears all three](scorecard_free.png)

Run these in order. The second a name fails one, you stop. You do not get to skip ahead because you like the story.

## Q1: which side of the 200-day, and which way is it pointed?

The 200-day moving average is not magic. It is the line where the people who move real size decide whether they are defending the stock or feeding you their shares.

Above a rising 200, pullbacks get bought and breakouts keep going. Below a falling 200, every rally is suspect until the line gets reclaimed. The slope matters as much as the price. A stock can sit above a 200 that is rolling over, or below one that is turning up. You want price above the line and the line pointed up. Both.

## Q2: are you at support, or at resistance?

Oversold at support, a pullback to a rising average or a real shelf, is where the sellers are tired and your risk is defined. Your stop sits right under the shelf. Overbought at resistance is the opposite trade entirely. You are buying from the exact people who are about to sell. Same stock, same week, and the math is upside down depending on where the candle is.

The gift is oversold in an uptrend. The trap is overbought into a wall.

## Q3: is the catalyst already in the price?

A catalyst only helps you if the tape has not reacted yet.

If everyone can see it, an index add, an earnings date, a launch, and the stock has already ripped into it on huge volume, you are not early. You are the exit. The tell is loud: a volume explosion, an overbought reading, and the news is the headline everyone is repeating. A live catalyst is the opposite. It is real, it is dated, and the tape is asleep on it. Buy the rumor nobody is buying yet.

Now watch the filter eat two charts that look perfectly fine.

## Case one: Disney fails at the first question

![DIS, ascending coil under a falling 200-day](dis.png)

DIS at $103.89 looks like it is waking up. Higher lows since March, a tidy little coil, a 3 percent pop. Tempting.

Then you ask Q1 and it is over. Price is sitting under a 200-day that is still sloping down, the moving average stack is bearish, and that coil is grinding straight into a lid around $105 to $107 where three different walls stack up at once. A coil into resistance below a falling 200 is not a reversal. It is a downtrend trying to become one and not there yet.

So you do not buy it — yet. You write down the level. The day DIS closes above $105 to $107 it reclaims the line and earns a second look. Until then it is a watch, not a trade. Failing Q1 means the other two questions never even get asked.

If you want the business reason behind what the chart is screaming, [a good macro write-up on Disney for 2026](https://www.accruedint.com/p/why-disney-stock-will-underperform) laid out the bear case, the TV-networks anchor and the YouTube problem. The tape and the fundamentals are telling the same story here.

## Case two: Rush Street passes the first question and fails the next two

![RSI, clean uptrend but overbought into the $30 wall](rsi.png)

RSI is the prettier chart. Up roughly 38 percent over its rising 200-day, full bullish stack, a genuine uptrend. It sails through Q1.

Then it falls apart. The stock is overbought, RSI reading north of 70, pressing right into a $30 wall. That is Q2, failed. And the catalyst, an S&P SmallCap 600 index add hitting this week, is already screaming out of the chart. Look at the volume on that last bar. Nearly nine times its average. That is index money front-running the add in plain sight. By the time you can read about a catalyst that clearly, it is in the price. Q3, failed.

Good chart. Spent setup. You do not chase it here. You wait for the pullback after the index buyers are done, somewhere back around $27 to $28, and you let it come to you.

Two clean-looking charts, two passes. The filter did its job before I lost a dime on either.
