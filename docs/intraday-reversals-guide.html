Reading the Turn

Spotting intraday reversals with technicals + options structure

A reversal is two questions answered at once: price tells you the turn is happening; dealer options positioning tells you whether it will stick or get run over. This guide pairs the classic technical tells with the gamma-flip lens — then proves both on real NQ tape.

Built & contributed by Chad, a mphinance reader. Thank you for building and sharing this.

01 — The price tells

What a reversal looks like on the chart

Everything below is a way of asking one thing: has supply/demand actually changed hands at this level, or is the crowd just pausing? No single bar is a signal — you want confluence, and you wait for the close to confirm (a wick poking through a level that closes back inside doesn't count).

Structure & context
  • V / inverted-V — a flush to a fresh low (or spike to a high) that fails and closes back the other way.
  • Failed breakout — a push beyond a level that can't hold = bull/bear trap; the reversal runs as trapped traders cover.
  • Double top / bottom — a re-test that refuses to make a new extreme.
  • Opening gap — unfilled after the first ~30 min tends to hold; a quick fill hints at exhaustion.
Trigger candles
  • Hammer / shooting star — long rejection wick (2–3× the body) at the extreme.
  • Engulfing / three-inside — a body that swallows the prior body; three-inside-up/down is the strongest-rated.
  • Doji — indecision after a trend, at a level, is the warning.
  • Read real bodies, not shadows, for support/resistance — and confirm on the close.
Volume & VWAP
  • Capitulation spike — a volume climax into the low/high is the crowd flushing out.
  • VWAP line-in-the-sand — a reclaim confirms a bottom; a rejection confirms a top.
  • Divergence — a new price extreme on lighter volume = weak conviction.
  • Beware the dead-cat bounce — a weak, low-volume rally after a drop continues lower ~81% of the time.
Tape timing
  • First hour sets tone as overnight news is absorbed; the last hour is repositioning.
  • The cleanest intraday reversals flush at a level mid-session, then turn.
  • Volume is heaviest at the open and close, thin midday — a midday climax stands out.
02 — The options lens

Why dealers decide whether a reversal holds

Market makers sell you what you buy and hedge the risk in futures — a forced, mechanical flow. Whether that hedging calms or feeds a move depends on the sign of dealer gamma, and the price where it flips sign — the zero-gamma level — is the single most useful number for reversals.

Above the flip · positive gamma
Dealers sell rallies, buy dips

Liquidity is injected, ranges compress, tape mean-reverts. Reversals stick — fading extremes back toward VWAP is the higher-probability trade.

Below the flip · negative gamma
Dealers buy highs, sell lows

Liquidity drains, moves get amplified, tape trends. Counter-trend fades get run over — wait for price to reclaim the flip before trusting a bottom.

Gamma walls = levels
  • Strikes with concentrated gamma act as magnets and barriers — natural reversal/pin levels.
  • A rejection at a wall in positive gamma is a clean fade; a break through one in negative gamma accelerates.
The options tape
  • Capitulation put buying into a low — aggressive, at-the-ask — is a contrarian exhaustion tell.
  • Watch the buy/sell aggression flip and put/call extremes; sentiment is contrarian at turns.

The move that matters: the flip cross itself is a trigger. Losing the zero-gamma level flips dealers from shock-absorbers to accelerants; reclaiming it does the reverse. Examples 3–4 below are exactly this, on consecutive days.

03 — Putting it together

A reversal confluence checklist

Rank the evidence in this order. The more boxes tick at the same level and the same time, the more the reversal is worth trading — and the last step tells you how hard to lean.

Context & level

Is there an actual trend to reverse, arriving at a level that matters — VWAP, a prior high/low, a gamma wall?

Trigger candle, confirmed on the close

Hammer, shooting star, engulfing or doji printing at that level — not a wick that closes back inside.

Volume signature

A capitulation climax into the extreme, or a divergence — the crowd flushing, not drifting.

VWAP resolution

Price reclaims VWAP (bottom) or gets rejected at it (top) — the intraday line in the sand.

Dealer regime — how hard to lean

Positive gamma above the flip: fading the extreme is safer. Negative gamma below it: don't catch the knife — wait for the flip reclaim.
