## 4. Quantitative Mechanics: Leverage, Lambda, and Indicators

### The Ladder of Learning Leverage
You must prove consistency at lower levels of leverage before "graduating" to higher risk.
*   **1x Leverage:** Standard shares (e.g., $NVDA). You must win a trade here first.
*   **2x Leverage:** Leveraged ETFs (e.g., $NVDL).
*   **High Leverage:** Options contracts (10x–30x leverage). 

### Lambda Calculations
Lambda ($\lambda$) represents the actual leverage of an option. While Delta shows price movement, Lambda shows the leverage ratio relative to your portfolio.

```text
λ = Delta * (Underlying Price / Option Price)
```
*Example:* Deciding between a $110 and $120 call on $NVDA is actually a choice between 13.5% leverage and 22.5% leverage. Use Lambda to ensure you aren't accidentally taking on more risk than your bankroll allows.

### Technical Confluences
The primary triggers for "Option Action" are the 5-day, 20-day, and 200-day EMAs:
*   **Bullish Signal:** Price > 20-day MA. **Ideal Entry:** Wait for the price to move *below* the 5-day EMA to sell your puts.
*   **Bearish Signal:** Price < 20-day MA. Sell calls when the price pops above the 5-day EMA.
