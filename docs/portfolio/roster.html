TASK FORCE GHOST — 30-Operator Squad

You're not managing 30 tickers. You're commanding three units. Every operator on the squad has one MOS, one watch rotation, and one rule for extraction. That's the ADHD win — you never stare at 30 undifferentiated tickers again, you stand one watch at a time. Thirty is the manpower cap: diversification math says ~20–30 names kills most single-stock risk, and past that you're just running a supply line you'll never maintain.

Deployment
Long-term core holds — blue chips, broad ETFs, compounders. The tanks. Dug in, hard to move.
The Mission
Hold the line and grind out the long war. They win the campaign whether you're watching the front or asleep in the bunker.
Watch

A THE ARTILLERY · Income Battery operators 12
Deployment
Income battery — the wheel: cash-secured put → assignment → covered call → reload. Steady barrage from behind the line.
The Mission
Rain premium every week and keep the task force funded. Methodical, lower-drama fire that pays for the whole operation.
Watch
MEDIUM · reload weekly  Same fire mission each week — roll and manage.
Extract when
Only fire on positions you'd be fine holding. If assignment would wreck you, it's not artillery — spike the gun.
Weight
Mid-weight; each gun ties up cash/shares as collateral — size for the assignment, not the premium.
R THE RAIDERS · Spec-Ops Fireteam operators 6 · capped
Deployment
Quick options — day & swing directional raids. A spec-ops fireteam: in fast, out fast.
The Mission
Hit hard and exfil. High risk, high reward. This is where the adrenaline lives — run it here, inside the wire, so it never touches Armor.
Watch
HIGH · eyes-on  But capped at a 6-operator fireteam so the loud unit can't lose the war.
Extract when
Time stop + hard stop, no exceptions. A raider who hasn't hit the objective by the clock exfils. No "one more push."
Weight
Smallest positions, defined risk per raid. Losing a raider stings; it never routs the task force.
🎖 THE RESERVES — QRF · Dry Powder & Watchlist (cash)

Not a deployed slot — it's the Quick Reaction Force staged in the rear. Cash plus your watchlist. You can't reinforce a broken front if you've got nobody in reserve, so keep powder. When Armor's thesis breaks or a gun goes cold, you deploy from here — not from a panicked market order.

🎯 RULES OF ENGAGEMENT — command doctrine for the scattered brain

The callsign only works if the discipline is real. Six ROE keep 30 positions from becoming 30 open tabs.
  1. Every operator has an MOS. If you can't name the role in one word — Armor, Artillery, Raider — it doesn't deploy. Kills the impulse buy at the wire.
  2. Cap the Raiders at 6. The loud unit is the one that loses wars. Leash the adrenaline or it burns the ammo the Artillery earned.
  3. One operator, one MOS. A ticker is Armor OR Artillery OR a Raider — never two. Role-creep is how a "raid" quietly digs into an 8-month foxhole.
  4. Different units, different watch rotations. Armor quarterly, Artillery weekly, Raiders daily. You never watch 30 things at once — you stand one watch on its schedule, then stand down.
  5. Weight by unit, not by nerve. Armor heaviest, Artillery mid, Raiders lightest. Conviction goes where the maintenance is lowest.
  6. 30 is the manpower cap. Recruiting a new name? Discharge an old one first. Past ~30 you get almost no diversification and a supply line your brain won't maintain.
Command the task force, not the chaos. — Sam the Quant Ghost 👻
