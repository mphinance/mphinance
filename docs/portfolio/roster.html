THE ROSTER — Your 30-Man Squad

You're not managing 30 tickers. You're managing three units. Every name on the squad has one job, one maintenance cadence, and one rule for when it gets benched. That's the ADHD win — you never stare at 30 undifferentiated tickers again, you check a unit at a time. Thirty is the salary cap: diversification math says ~20–30 names kills most single-stock risk, and past that you're just opening tabs you'll never look at.

C THE SPINE · Captains roster slots 12
Position
Long-term core holds — buy-and-hold compounders, blue chips, broad ETFs. The franchise players.
The Job
Compound quietly and anchor the whole book. This is the score at the end of the season — they carry the team whether you're watching or not.
Maintenance
LOW · check quarterly  Set-and-forget. The unit built for the scattered brain.
Sub-out rule
You bench a captain on a broken thesis, never a red day. Story changes → cut. Price dips → hold the line.
Size
Biggest positions on the squad. This is where your capital lives.
W THE ENGINE ROOM · Wheelers roster slots 12
Position
Income engine — the wheel: cash-secured put → assignment → covered call → repeat. Possession midfielders.
The Job
Generate cash every week and control the tempo. Lower drama than directional bets — they pay the bills while the captains compound and the strikers gamble.
Maintenance
MEDIUM · weekly rolls  Your standing training session — same day each week, roll and manage.
Sub-out rule
Only wheel names you'd be happy to own. If assignment would make you sick, it's not a wheeler — cut it before it cuts you.
Size
Mid-weight, and remember each one ties up cash/shares as collateral — size for the assignment, not the premium.
ST THE STRIKE FORCE · Impact Subs roster slots 6 · capped
Position
Quick options — day & swing directional plays. Strikers who come on to score fast, then off.
The Job
Score in bursts. High risk, high reward, in and out. This is also where the dopamine lives — feed the ADHD brain here, on a leash, so it never touches the core.
Maintenance
HIGH · intraday  Watched closely — but strictly capped at 6 slots so the fun unit can't sink the season.
Sub-out rule
Time stop + hard stop, no exceptions. A striker that hasn't scored by the whistle comes off. No "just one more day."
Size
Smallest positions, defined risk per bet. Losing a striker should sting, never bench the whole team.
🪑 THE BENCH — Dry Powder & Watchlist (cash)

Not a roster slot — it's the subs warming up on the sideline. Cash plus your watchlist. You can't make a substitution if there's nobody on the bench, so keep powder. When a captain's thesis breaks or a wheeler goes cold, you sub in from here — not from a panicked market order.

📋 COACH'S RULES — roster management for the scattered brain

The metaphor only works if the discipline is real. Six rules keep 30 positions from becoming 30 open tabs.
  1. Every position has a job title. If you can't name its role in one word — Captain, Wheeler, Striker — it doesn't make the squad. Kills the impulse buy at the door.
  2. Cap the strikers at 6. The fun unit is the one that blows up accounts. Leash the thing that gives you the dopamine, or it eats the paycheck the wheelers earned.
  3. One player, one position. A ticker is EITHER a captain OR a wheel OR a striker — never two. Role-mixing is how a "day trade" quietly becomes an 8-month bag.
  4. Different units, different cadences. Captains quarterly, wheelers weekly, strikers daily. You're never watching 30 things at once — you check one unit on its schedule and close the laptop.
  5. Size by unit, not by vibe. Captains biggest, wheelers middle, strikers smallest. The conviction goes where the maintenance is lowest.
  6. 30 is the salary cap. Want to sign a new name? Bench an old one first. Past ~30 you get almost no diversification benefit and a pile of admin your brain will skip.
Manage the squad, not the chaos. — Sam the Quant Ghost 👻
