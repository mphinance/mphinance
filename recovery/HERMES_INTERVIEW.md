# Hermes interview brief: the Recovery Nav article

> How to use: tell Hermes "read https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mphinance/mphinance/main/recovery/HERMES_INTERVIEW.md and interview me about it by voice." Hermes runs the interview, then drafts the Substack post for Michael to approve. Hermes never publishes anything.

## What this is

Michael is building a free, neutral guide to substance use treatment and recovery in Southeast Wisconsin, with the idea of growing it into something counties can fund. The code is public (MIT): https://github.com/mphinance/recovery-nav. The name is a placeholder.

The goal of this interview is one Substack article that (1) tells the story in his own voice and (2) asks readers for ideas, corrections and introductions. It is not a product launch and not a pitch deck.

Before drafting, read `VOICE.md` and `SUBSTACK.md` in this repo. They are the source of truth for voice, formatting and the sign-off. Where this brief and those files disagree, they win.

## How to run the interview (voice)

- One question at a time. Keep each question under 20 words. Then stop and listen.
- Let him ramble. Do not interrupt a story to ask the next question. Follow up on the best detail in what he just said ("what did that look like?", "who was in the room?").
- Every few questions, say back what you heard in two sentences and ask what you got wrong.
- Do not write the article while he talks. Take notes of exact phrases worth quoting, with his wording unchanged.
- If he says skip, skip. If he says off the record, mark it and do not use it.
- Plain speech. No therapy voice, no hype, no cheerleading.

## The arc (about 12 questions; reorder freely)

1. Why this, why now? What happened that made you want to build it?
2. Picture the person typing "rehab near me" at 2am. Who are they, and what happens next?
3. What did you see go wrong in how people get steered to treatment?
4. How did you find your own way in? Say only what you want public. Your call every time.
5. What is the one thing a directory can never tell a family that a person who has been through it can?
6. What does the guide actually do today? (He should describe it; the facts below are for checking.)
7. Why free, and why no referral fees, ever?
8. Why counties? What did you learn about the settlement money?
9. What scares you about doing this in public?
10. Who should be in this with you? Who have you not talked to yet?
11. What would make you quit, and what would make you double down?
12. What do you want readers to do after they finish?

## Public facts he can use (verify before publishing)

- Wisconsin local governments received about $162.6M in opioid settlement money from 2022 through 2025 and had reported spending about $44.1M of it, leaving about $125.4M unspent at the end of 2025. Source: Wisconsin Watch settlement tracker data. Check the current figures first.
- The state license directory (Wisconsin Department of Health Services) lists licensed substance use providers but has no beds, no payment information and no map data. The guide adds a map, plain-English levels of care and a plain "what to ask when you call" list. Signed facility reviews are a later step, not part of the first release.
- The code and method are in the public repo above. Anything else about plans, targets or conversations in progress is private and stays out of the article.

## Boundaries (do not cross these)

- **No fellowship branding.** Never say or imply that AA, NA or any fellowship endorses, runs or is affiliated with this. Michael speaks as an individual. Do not name other members or describe their stories; if he tells one, strip identifying details and ask before using it.
- **No claims about a specific facility** unless Michael saw it first-hand or it comes from a cited public source. No accusations. Do not describe any facility review as published, planned for a date, or ready.
- **No promises about the product.** It is early. Say what exists today and what is only an idea. Opinion is fine when it is labelled as his and he discloses any connection (for example, if he is asked by a facility to review it).
- **No medical advice,** no "this will work for you," and no outcomes promises. Include 988 for anyone in crisis.
- **Nothing private.** No named officials, outreach plans, draft pricing, API keys, internal notes or anything from the private research. If unsure, leave it out and ask.
- No health information about anyone, ever.

## The article

- Follow `SUBSTACK.md` for layout, the subtitle format (a category mix such as `*Mindset 50% | Business 30% | Research 20%*`) and the sign-off from `VOICE.md`. No em dashes. No markdown tables; use an image if a table is needed.
- Open with the strongest, most honest take from the interview, not a throat-clearing intro.
- Short paragraphs. His words, lightly cleaned. Mark any figure with its source.
- Include a closing section titled something like "Help me build this" with specific asks:
  - What should a guide like this never do?
  - Which programs or services in Southeast Wisconsin should be in it?
  - What would you want to know before calling a facility for someone you love?
  - Who should I be talking to (counties, programs, people in recovery, clinicians)?
  - What should it be called?
  - Would you use it, and would you tell someone about it?
  Tell readers how to reply (Substack comment or reply to the email) and that corrections are welcome.
- Save the cleaned transcript and the draft together in the article directory, and give Michael the draft to approve. Hermes does not post, schedule or send anything.
