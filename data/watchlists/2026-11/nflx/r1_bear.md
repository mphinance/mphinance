# NFLX Round 1 — BEAR case (technicals / price structure / options positioning)
*Data: dossier (TDPro + yfinance, Fri 10/30 close $62.08) plus fresh pulls below.*

## The 5 strongest bear arguments

**1. This is a textbook accelerating downtrend, not exhaustion — and I have the base-rate data to back it up.**
ADX 31.2 and *rising*, -DI 39.2 crushing +DI 14.8. That combination doesn't mean "oversold, due for a bounce" — it means trend strength is still building and sellers are firmly in control. RSI pinned at 30-31 for four straight sessions is exactly what RSI does *inside* a strong downtrend (it doesn't need to make new lows to confirm more downside — it just needs to not turn up). I pulled every day in NFLX's last 5 years where it hit this same regime (ADX>28, -DI>35, RSI<33 — 56 sessions total, including 2022's crash and this summer's slide). Forward 20-day return averaged **-1.6%**, only **44.6%** of instances were net positive, and the average *further drawdown* before any bounce (`fwdmin20`) was **-11.8%**. Even the episodes that eventually worked (July 2026: +13-17% over 20-40 days) had to survive a leg lower first. The base rate says: expect more pain before relief, not "Friday was the low."

**2. Negative gamma isn't a cushion, it's an accelerant — and the whole ladder below spot is negative.**
Pulled the live GEX/VEX ladder: every strike from $57 to $63 carries *negative* net gamma, with $60 the single largest concentration (-$27.3M, vexRelative -100, STRONG alignment) and $58 the next-biggest (-$11.2M). Dealers are short gamma here. That means as price falls *toward* $60/$58, dealers are forced to sell into the decline to stay hedged — the opposite of the long-gamma "buy the dip, sell the rip" dynamic that makes a level a floor. These strikes are magnets because of OI concentration, but once touched in a negative-gamma regime they're just as likely to get run through as to hold. Calling $60/$58 "support" is backwards: it's where the next leg down gets fuel, not where it stops. The flip only sits at $64.38-65.97 — comfortably *above* spot — so the whole current trading zone is in destabilizing territory.

**3. Seven-day dark pool tape is distribution, not accumulation, and it's flagged HIGH.**
Net -$114M over the last week (-$78.7M bought vs -$193M sold — roughly 2.5:1 sell-to-buy), and TDPro's own alert composite reads "HIGH." Someone with size is working out of this name patiently while price chops sideways-to-down. That's consistent with a slow distribution top, not smart money building a position into a bottom. It also explains why the stock can't rally even on a "flat" day — there's a steady offer underneath it.

**4. Structural picture: every lower high, no nearby support, nothing but air below the 52-week low.**
EMA8/21/50, SMA50/100/200 are all stacked bearishly and sloping down. Beta to SPY is 0.22 (effectively uncorrelated) and the stock is -31.8% over 6 months while SPY is +5.8% — this is an idiosyncratic breakdown, not beta risk-off that reverses when the tape calms down. The weekly swing-high sequence since the Sep 2025 peak is a clean staircase down: 108.9 → 91.5 → 78.4 → 83.6 → 81.0 → 73.1 → 70.7 → 65.5. Every single bounce has topped lower than the last. The "trendline" plan's own trigger (close > $68-69) is just the next rung of that same ladder — there's no evidence yet that this pattern is done. Below: the stock printed a fresh 52-week low Thursday (61.39) and closed basically on top of it Friday. I went back through the full price history — there is **no real support shelf between here and the Jan-Aug 2024 basing range** (lows of $55.06 in Apr '24, $59.86 in Aug '24), which is another ~10% below spot. The $60/$58 GEX levels sit *inside* that air pocket, not at a historically defended price.

**5. The trade plans are priced for a trend-reversal that the tape hasn't earned.**
- Trendline plan: TP1 is 0.9R against a $64.80 stop and $68-69 trigger. That's a sub-1:1 first target on a name that just made a new 52-week low — you need an unusually high win rate just to break even, and the base-rate work above says the win rate here is closer to 45% than "high."
- Early plan: the $66 trigger sits *right on top of* both the call wall ($65) and the gamma flip ($64.38-65.97). That's precisely the kind of level that produces a one-day poke-and-fail — a short-covering/gamma-squeeze wick through resistance that reverses once dealers re-hedge on the other side, rather than a clean regime change. Worth flagging: ATM IV crept from 30.8% to 33.9% over the last five sessions *while price went nowhere*. Vol creeping up under a flat tape is hedging/put demand building quietly, not a "calm before the breakout" signal.

## What's genuinely strong (concede)
- **Friday's price action was real relative strength**: NFLX +0.05% vs SPY -1.77% on a bad macro day (Intel guidance, AI-capex scare) is not nothing — the stock didn't crack on a risk-off tape.
- **Nearest-weekly put/call ratio is 0.35, labeled bullish** (62K calls vs 22K puts, Nov 6 expiry) — near-term flow is not uniformly bearish. Caveat: this is volume on the single nearest weekly, which is exactly the kind of print that fills with cheap speculative lottery calls and covered-call overwrite supply on a beaten-down, low-IV-rank name — it doesn't obviously square with a market that's short gamma and distributing in the dark pool, so I'd weight it below the positioning/flow data above, but it's a real data point against the thesis.
- **IV rank 20.6 is genuinely cheap** — if you're buying calls rather than shares, the convexity is cheap and the plan's January-call idea limits tail risk versus holding stock into whatever comes next.
- No unusual options activity flagged — this isn't a name where someone is loading up ahead of a known catalyst with size.

## The level that breaks the bear case
A **daily close back above ~$66** (through both the call wall and the gamma flip) on volume meaningfully above the recent 1.1-1.5x-average pace, ideally with RSI breaking its 30-31 pin and turning up through 40+, would flip dealer hedging positive-gamma and open room toward $68-72. A **weekly close above the falling trendline (~$69)** with that kind of volume/RSI confirmation is the real "the downtrend is over" signal — not a single flat Friday into a known put wall.

## Bear conviction: 7/10
Strong trend, negative gamma all the way down, distribution tape, bad R:R on the trendline plan, and no nearby support below the 52-week low all argue against initiating long here. Not a 9-10 because the options flow (bullish P/C, cheap IV) and Friday's relative strength are real, if weaker, counter-signals, and Stranger Things S5 (Nov 26) is a legitimate, dated catalyst inside the hold window that could change the flow picture fast.
