# NFLX Round 2 — BULL case (fundamentals / news / catalysts / macro)
Data: dossier (10/30/26 close $62.08) + live web search 10/31/26. All dollar figures are current (post 10-for-1 split, effective 11/17/25) unless noted.

## Rebutting the bear's Round 1 case

**1. The 56-session backtest — the sample is clustered, not independent, and that matters.**
Bear's quote: *"I pulled every day in NFLX's last 5 years where it hit this same regime (ADX>28, -DI>35, RSI<33 — 56 sessions total)... Forward 20-day return averaged -1.6%, only 44.6% of instances were net positive."*

This is a real tell, but the "56 sessions" framing overstates its own precision. ADX>28/-DI>35/RSI<33 is a *persistence* condition — once a downtrend trips it, the regime typically stays tripped for many consecutive sessions (that's what trend strength means), and bear's own examples are "2022's crash and this summer's slide" — i.e., two or three multi-week episodes, not 56 independent events. Two consequences: (a) effective sample size is probably 4-8 independent *episodes*, not 56 independent trials, so the 44.6%/-1.6% stats carry much wider real uncertainty than they look; (b) the forward-20-day windows for adjacent sessions in the same episode overlap by ~95%, so a single bad episode (e.g., 2022) gets counted and re-counted dozens of times in both the trigger count and the forward-return average, which can make one historical crash look like "the base rate" when it's one event with a long tail. I can't re-run the backtest (no data access this round), so I can't say the sign is wrong — the direction (expect chop, not a clean V) probably survives episode-counting. But I'd cut confidence in the precise numbers hard, and I'd want episode-level (not session-level) stats before trusting 44.6% as more than "roughly a coin flip, maybe worse." **Partial concession: direction stands, precision doesn't.**

**2. Negative gamma ladder, dark pool distribution, structural staircase-down — concede these.** These are flow/technical points outside this round's lens and I'm not going to pretend fundamentals data overturns options positioning. Worth noting on magnitude only: -$114M net dark-pool flow over 7 days is ~0.04% of NFLX's ~$258B market cap — real, and directionally bad, but not an overwhelming print by itself.

**3. The trade-plan R:R critique is fair but orthogonal to the fundamental case.** Bear is right that a sub-1:1 first target on a fresh 52-week low needs a high win rate to pay. The fundamental argument below isn't "buy because the chart says so" — it's that the de-rating itself (forward P/E cut roughly in half from its own 5-year average — see below) is doing some of the stop-loss-shaped work a technical support level would otherwise need to do. That's a different kind of margin of safety, not a rebuttal of the chart read.

## The fundamental long case

**Valuation after the 51% drawdown.** At $62.08, against current sell-side consensus EPS of roughly $3.54-3.58 (FY26) and $3.82-3.83 (FY27) — figures that mostly pre-date the 10/20 Q4-guide cut and may come down a touch more — NFLX trades around **17.4x forward (FY26e) and 16.2x (FY27e) earnings**. A July 2026 note (SentiSense, post-Q2 drop) already called 19-20x cheap against a **5-year average near 36x**; today's price is lower still, so the derating from "growth media darling" multiple to "mature compounder" multiple looks mostly done, not half-done. EV/FCF: back-of-envelope using the 2026 FCF guide (~$12.0B) and a market cap of ~$258B (62.08 × ~4.16B shares) plus modest net debt gives **~22x EV/FCF** — this is my own calculation, not a single clean sourced figure, so treat it as approximate. That's still a premium to Warner Bros. Discovery's ~13x (mid-2025 reference point, since stale) and Paramount's even-more-stretched FCF profile — but WBD and Paramount carry ~4-4.6x leverage and shrinking businesses; NFLX has low leverage, a growing (if decelerating) top line, and is the only name in the legacy-media/streaming set that isn't structurally challenged. Paying up for the one clean balance sheet in a levered peer group is a defensible premium, not obviously a bubble multiple.

**Buyback.** Netflix repurchased **$4.8B of stock in Q3 2026 — the largest buyback quarter in company history** — while the stock was falling, with **$22.3B still authorized**. That's roughly 8% of current market cap in dry powder, deployed at prices far below where management was buying a year ago. This is a real, ongoing EPS tailwind independent of subscriber growth.

**FCF — the cut is a timing item, not a demand miss.** 2026 FCF guide was trimmed to ~$12.0B from ~$12.5B, but the company attributed this specifically to the **timing of a real-estate transaction closing in Q4**, not to content spend or subscriber weakness, and said capital allocation is unchanged. A bear reading "another guide cut" as operational deterioration is over-reading a line-item that's mostly a quarter-shift, not a trend change.

**Ad business — concede the miss, but it's a pace problem, not a thesis problem.** 2026 ad-revenue guide was cut to ~$2.7B from ~$3B (~10%), driven by slower partner-integration rollout — this is real and I'm not going to spin it away. But: the shortfall is explicitly because subscription revenue is running *ahead* of plan and covering the gap, not because ad demand disappeared; ad-tier sign-ups were >60% of new signups in available markets as of Q1, advertiser base grew >70% YoY to 4,000+ clients, and the tier expands to 15 more countries starting March 2027. Read as execution-speed slippage inside a still-growing book, not a reversal.

**Pricing power.** Netflix raised **all U.S. plan prices again on 3/25-26/26** (ad tier $7.99→$8.99, Standard $17.99→$19.99, Premium $24.99→$26.99) — the second broad hike in about a year, funding a 2026 content budget raised to $20B from $18B. No evidence in the Q3 print that this triggered elevated subscriber churn (the Q4 miss was framed around ad-revenue timing and margin from the 1,000-person layoff, not sub losses). That's real, exercised pricing power.

**Live sports / NFL.** The NFL deal was expanded in May 2026 and now runs **through the 2029-30 season**, adding — on top of Christmas Day games — an annual Week 1 game, a Thanksgiving Eve game, a Week 18 finale, and NFL Honors. The **Thanksgiving Eve game (Packers @ Rams, Nov 25, 2026) sits inside Michael's hold window** and is a real, dated, verified live-programming event, even if it's a small one relative to a tentpole drama drop.

**Stranger Things S5 "Nov 26" catalyst — this needs a hard correction, not a bull point.** I verified the actual release schedule: **Stranger Things Season 5 Volume 1 released November 26, 2025 — Volume 2 December 25, 2025, and the finale December 31, 2025** (New Year's Day viewership record, confirmed). That is a full calendar year in the past, not an upcoming November 2026 catalyst. The dossier's "verified Nov catalyst" line appears to be a stale/misdated carryover — **there is no Stranger Things drop in November 2026.** I checked the actual Nov-2026 Netflix slate: Black Doves S2 (Nov 5), Love Is Blind Reunion (Nov 8), The Altruists (Nov 19), Hunting Wives S2 (Nov 26 — note the same calendar date, different show), Great British Baking Show Holidays (Nov 23), assorted Christmas films, Spider-Man: Brand New Day (licensed title). None of these has anywhere near Stranger Things' viewership gravity. **This is a real negative for the "idiosyncratic catalyst inside the window" argument** — I'm flagging it rather than quietly dropping it.

**Historical check: did big Stranger Things drops actually move the stock, and when?** Yes, twice, verified — but **always at the next earnings report, not on air date.** S4 (2022): Q2'22 earnings (reported 7/19/22) beat the company's own subscriber-loss guidance, which Reed Hastings credited partly to Stranger Things 4; stock jumped roughly 6-8% that day. S5 (2025-26): Q4'25 earnings (reported 1/20/26) beat on a strong holiday quarter (15B minutes watched on ST5 in December), and the stock reportedly jumped ~15%. Both moves happened when the engagement got *quantified in a subscriber/revenue print*, weeks to months after the actual release. **Implication for this trade: even a real tentpole content catalyst doesn't re-rate the stock until the next earnings call — which for NFLX is ~late January 2027, outside Michael's ~1-month November window.** That's a genuine strike against trading November for a content-driven pop, tentpole or not.

**Beta/correlation as ballast into a hawkish December, not a catalyst.** 0.22 beta / 0.07 correlation to SPX, with a December 8-9 FOMC hike priced 75-90% per the dossier. If that hawkish setup drives a broad multiple-compression selloff into year-end, NFLX is structurally one of the least likely names in the existing debate slate (EQT, NVDA, USB, TXN) to get dragged down mechanically with it — its price action is almost entirely idiosyncratic (flows, GEX, its own news), not beta. That argues for NFLX as a **diversifier position sized for ballast**, not for a specific "December hike helps NFLX" mechanism — and it cuts both ways, since it also won't get a relief-rally lift if risk sentiment improves. Same point bear made in Round 1; I'm not contesting it, just reframing it as portfolio construction rather than catalyst.

**Post-10/20 analyst reaction — this is the one genuinely new bullish data point this round.** Verified a long list of post-Q3 moves: UBS (Buy, cut to $100-110 depending on source), Bank of America (Buy, cut $105→$90), Morgan Stanley (Overweight, cut $80→$77), Guggenheim (Buy, cut $80→$78), MoffettNathanson (Buy, cut $100→$90), Seaport (Buy, cut $90→$85), Piper Sandler (Overweight, cut to $78), Rosenblatt (Neutral, cut $75→$71), Jefferies (Buy, $85), Deutsche Bank (Buy, $93), BMO (Outperform, held $125, explicitly citing the long-term ad thesis as intact). **Only Needham downgraded (Buy→Hold).** Virtually the entire sell-side cut numbers hard but kept constructive ratings — treating this as a guidance reset, not a broken thesis. Remaining targets cluster $71-125 against a $62 spot, i.e., Street sees real upside even post-cut. Caveat, and it's a real one: these same analysts have cut targets nearly every quarter as the stock fell all year, so the rating-persistence signal is worth more than the absolute PT math, which has been serially wrong on the way down.

## Updated bull conviction: 6/10 (up from 5)

What earned the point: a genuinely cheap print (~17x forward earnings, roughly half its own 5-year multiple), a record buyback funded by real cash flow (FCF cut is a timing item, not demand), real and recently-exercised pricing power, a structurally extended NFL deal, and — the single new finding this round — near-unanimous sell-side conviction holding (ratings, not targets) straight through the Q3 miss.

What kept it from going higher: I have to formally retract the round's marquee catalyst — Stranger Things S5 already aired a year ago, and there is no comparable tentpole drop in the actual November 2026 slate, which removes the "why trade November specifically" hook. Worse, the historical pattern shows content catalysts in this name only get priced at the *next earnings report* (late Jan 2027), outside the hold window entirely. The ad-revenue miss is a real, un-spun execution problem, and I'm conceding the bear's flow/technical case in full (negative gamma, dark-pool distribution, no nearby support) since fundamentals don't override positioning in a one-month window. Net: cheap and well-capitalized, but with no internal-November fundamental trigger to force a re-rating inside Michael's horizon — a valuation/quality case for owning it into January earnings, not a high-conviction case for this specific month.
